Tennis Rules & How to Play

The Basics

  • Tennis is played between two players (singles) or four players (doubles)
  • The objective is to hit the ball over the net into the opponent's court
  • The ball must bounce once before being returned
  • Players alternate serves each game
  • The first player to win 6 games wins a set (must win by 2)
  • Matches are best of 3 or 5 sets

Scoring System

  • Points: 0 (Love), 15, 30, 40, Game
  • Deuce: When score is 40-40
  • Advantage: One point after deuce
  • Game: Win by 2 points after deuce
  • Set: First to 6 games (win by 2)
  • Tiebreak: At 6-6, first to 7 points (win by 2)

The Court

  • Dimensions: 78 feet (23.77m) long
  • Singles width: 27 feet (8.23m)
  • Doubles width: 36 feet (10.97m)
  • Net height: 3 feet (0.914m) at center
  • Service boxes on each side
  • Baseline and service lines

Serving Rules

  • Server stands behind baseline
  • Serve diagonally to opponent's service box
  • Two serve attempts allowed
  • Foot fault if stepping on/over baseline
  • Let: Serve touches net but lands in - replay
  • Alternate sides after each point

Key Rules

  • Ball must land inside the lines (lines are in)
  • Can't touch net with racket or body
  • Ball can't bounce twice on your side
  • Can't hit ball before it crosses net
  • Ball can only be hit once per return
  • Challenges allowed (Hawk-Eye system)

Grand Slam Specifics

  • Men play best of 5 sets
  • Women play best of 3 sets
  • Final set rules vary by tournament
  • Australian Open: 10-point super tiebreak at 6-6 in final set
  • French Open: No tiebreak in final set
  • Wimbledon: Tiebreak at 12-12 in final set
  • US Open: Regular tiebreak at 6-6 in final set

About the Australian Open

The Australian Open is the first of the four Grand Slam tennis tournaments held each year. Played on hard courts at Melbourne Park, it's known for its extreme heat, vibrant blue courts, and passionate crowds. The tournament moved to its current location in 1988 and has since grown to become one of the world's premier sporting events.


Surface: Plexicushion hard court (blue)
Venue: Melbourne Park
Prize Money: $96.5 million AUD (2026)
Winner's Prize: $4.15 million AUD each (singles)

About Roland Garros

The French Open, also called Roland Garros, is the premier clay court championship in the world and the only Grand Slam tournament currently held on this surface. The slow-playing surface and five-set matches (for men) make it one of the most physically demanding tournaments. The distinctive red clay courts and Parisian atmosphere make it one of tennis's most iconic events.


Surface: Red clay
Venue: Stade Roland Garros
Prize Money: €53.5 million (2026 estimated)
Unique Feature: No final set tiebreak - play continues until 2-game advantage

About Wimbledon

Wimbledon is the oldest tennis tournament in the world and is widely regarded as the most prestigious. It is the only Grand Slam still played on grass courts, the game's original surface. Famous for its traditions including the strict all-white dress code, royal patronage, strawberries and cream, and the lack of sponsor advertising around the courts.


Surface: Grass
Venue: All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club
Prize Money: £50 million (2026 estimated)
Traditions: All-white dress code, Centre Court queue, strawberries & cream
Royal Box: Members of the British Royal Family traditionally attend

About the US Open

The US Open is the fourth and final Grand Slam tournament of the year, played on hard courts in New York City. Known for its electric atmosphere, especially during night sessions under the lights of Arthur Ashe Stadium. The tournament is famous for its loud, enthusiastic crowds and being the highest-attended annual sporting event in the world.

Venue: USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center
Prize Money: $75 million (2026 estimated)
Main Stadium: Arthur Ashe Stadium (capacity 23,771 - largest tennis stadium)
Unique Feature: Tiebreak at 6-6 in final set

About the Rankings

ATP and WTA rankings are updated weekly on Mondays and are based on a rolling 52-week system. Players earn points based on their performance in tournaments, with Grand Slams offering the most points.

Rankings determine seeding for tournaments and qualification for year-end championships. The top 8 singles players and doubles teams qualify for the ATP Finals and WTA Finals respectively.
